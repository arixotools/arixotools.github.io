About the WebP to JPG Converter

WebP is a modern image format used by many websites because it makes pictures smaller. The problem is that some older apps, printers, email programs, government portals and upload forms still refuse WebP files. This converter turns WebP images into ordinary JPG files that open nearly anywhere. The conversion runs inside your browser, so your images are never uploaded.

How to use the WebP to JPG Converter

  1. Click Choose image files and select one or more WebP images.
  2. Adjust the quality slider if you want a smaller or sharper file.
  3. Pick a background color in case your image has transparent areas.
  4. Click Convert to JPG and download each result.

Where it is useful

  • Saving images from websites that download as WebP.
  • Uploading pictures to forms and portals that only accept JPG.
  • Opening WebP files in older editing software.
  • Sending photos by email or messaging apps that do not show WebP.

WebP versus JPG

CompressionUsually smaller files at the same lookSlightly larger files

If your WebP has a transparent background, JPG cannot keep it. Those areas are filled with the background color you choose, white by default. If you need to keep transparency, convert to PNG instead.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use 85 to 95% quality for photos. Lower values make the file smaller but may add visible blocks.
  • Converting from a lossy format loses a little quality each time, so keep the original.
  • For logos or screenshots with sharp text, PNG is usually a better choice than JPG.
  • Large images may take a moment, so be patient with batches.

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert WebP to JPG?

Choose your WebP files, click Convert to JPG and download the results.

Is the conversion private?

Yes. It happens in your browser and nothing is uploaded.

Will quality be lost?

A little, as JPG is lossy. Using a high quality setting keeps it small enough to notice.

Can I convert many files at once?

Yes, up to 30 images at a time.

What happens to transparency?

JPG cannot store it, so transparent areas get the background color you pick.
