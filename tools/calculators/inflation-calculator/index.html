Inflation Calculator

See how inflation raises future costs and shrinks the value of money.

About the Inflation Calculator

Inflation is the steady rise in prices. It means the same amount of money buys less each year, which is why savings that just sit idle slowly lose value. This calculator shows two things: what something that costs a given amount today could cost in the future, and what a given amount will be worth in today's terms after the years pass.

How to use the Inflation Calculator

  1. Enter the amount you are thinking about, in today's rupees.
  2. Enter the average yearly inflation you expect.
  3. Enter the number of years.
  4. Click Calculate to see the future cost and the loss of purchasing power.

Where it is useful

  • Estimating the cost of education, weddings or healthcare years from now.
  • Checking whether your savings return beats inflation.
  • Planning a retirement corpus that keeps its buying power.
  • Understanding why a fixed monthly expense will rise over time.

Formula and example

Future cost = Amount × (1 + inflation)years
Value in today's terms = Amount ÷ (1 + inflation)years

At 6% inflation for 10 years, something that costs ₹1,000 today would cost about ₹1,790.85, and ₹1,000 kept as cash would buy only about ₹558.39 worth of today's goods.

Real return

If your investment earns 8% and inflation is 6%, your real return is only about 2%. Compare returns with inflation, not just with zero. The CAGR Calculator helps you find the actual yearly rate your money has grown.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use a realistic average. Inflation varies by year and by item, and education or healthcare costs often rise faster than the general rate.
  • Try two rates, such as 5% and 7%, to see a range.
  • Remember that inflation compounds, so a few years of small increases add up.
  • This is an estimate. Actual future prices cannot be predicted.

Frequently asked questions

What is inflation?

It is the general rise in prices over time, which reduces what each unit of money can buy.

What inflation rate should I use?

A long-term average for your country is a reasonable start. Try a few values to see how sensitive the result is.

How do I beat inflation?

Aim for returns above the inflation rate after tax. Investments differ in risk, so consider professional advice.

What does value in today's terms mean?

It is how much of today's buying power a future amount of money represents.

Is the result accurate?

It is a projection based on the rate you enter. Real inflation changes from year to year.
