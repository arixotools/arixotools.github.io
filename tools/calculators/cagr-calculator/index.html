About the CAGR Calculator

CAGR, or Compound Annual Growth Rate, is the steady yearly rate at which an investment would have to grow to move from its starting value to its ending value over a period. It smooths out ups and downs, so it is the standard way to compare mutual funds, stocks, business revenue or any other figure that changes over several years.

How to use the CAGR Calculator

  1. Enter the value at the start.
  2. Enter the value at the end.
  3. Enter the number of years between them.
  4. Click Calculate to see the CAGR, the total growth and the growth multiple.

Where it is useful

  • Comparing the performance of two funds over the same period.
  • Checking how fast your savings or portfolio has really grown.
  • Measuring business metrics such as revenue or users.
  • Turning a past result into a yearly rate to compare with a bank deposit.

Formula and example

CAGR = (Ending value ÷ Starting value)1 ÷ years − 1

If ₹1,00,000 becomes ₹2,00,000 in 5 years, the CAGR is about 14.87% a year. The money doubled, but the yearly rate is not 20%, because growth compounds.

CAGR versus simple average return

If an investment gains 50% one year and loses 50% the next, the simple average is 0%, but you have actually lost 25% of your money. CAGR reflects that reality, which is why it is preferred for multi-year comparisons.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Use the same period when comparing two CAGRs.
  • CAGR hides volatility. Two investments with the same CAGR can feel very different along the way.
  • It ignores money added or withdrawn mid-way. For regular investments, XIRR is more accurate.
  • A short period can give a misleading CAGR.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good CAGR?

It depends on the asset and risk. Compare it with inflation and with alternatives such as fixed deposits for the same period.

Can CAGR be negative?

Yes. If the ending value is lower than the starting value the result is negative, but this calculator needs both values above zero.

Is CAGR the same as average return?

No. CAGR accounts for compounding, while a simple average does not.

Can I use CAGR for SIP returns?

Not accurately, because SIP money goes in at different times. XIRR is better for that.

Is CAGR a guarantee?

No. It describes the past, not what will happen next.
