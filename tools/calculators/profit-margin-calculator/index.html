About the Profit Margin Calculator

Margin and markup both describe profit, but they use different bases, and confusing them is one of the most expensive pricing mistakes in small business. Margin is profit as a share of the selling price, while markup is profit as a share of the cost. This calculator shows both from your cost and price, or works backwards to the selling price needed for a target margin.

How to use the Profit Margin Calculator

  1. Enter your cost price.
  2. Enter the selling price to see profit, margin and markup.
  3. Or leave the selling price empty and enter a target margin to find the price you should charge.
  4. Click Calculate.

Where it is useful

  • Pricing products for a shop, online store or reseller business.
  • Checking whether a discount still leaves a healthy margin.
  • Comparing the profitability of different products.
  • Quoting a price for services or freelance work with a target margin.

Formulas

Margin % = (Selling price − Cost) ÷ Selling price × 100
Markup % = (Selling price − Cost) ÷ Cost × 100
Price for a target margin = Cost ÷ (1 − margin)

Tips and common mistakes

  • A 50% markup is not a 50% margin. It gives a 33.3% margin.
  • Margin can never reach 100%, while markup can go above 100%.
  • Include all costs, such as packaging, shipping and fees, in the cost price.
  • Check margins after GST and discounts, not before.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit divided by selling price. Markup is profit divided by cost.

How do I price for a 30% margin?

Divide the cost by 0.70. For a cost of ₹700, the price is ₹1,000.

What is a good profit margin?

It varies widely by business. Compare against others in your industry.

Is gross margin the same as net margin?

No. Gross margin uses only direct costs. Net margin subtracts all expenses, tax and interest.

Does this include GST?

No. Use pre-GST prices, or use the GST Calculator first.
