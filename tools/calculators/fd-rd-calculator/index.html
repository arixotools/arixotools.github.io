FD and RD calculator for Indian bank deposits

A fixed deposit (FD) locks one lump sum for a set period at a fixed interest rate. A recurring deposit (RD) lets you save a fixed amount every month and earn interest on each instalment for the time it stays in the account. Both are popular low-risk ways to save, and this calculator handles them in one place so you can compare maturity amounts side by side.

Most Indian banks compound deposit interest quarterly, so that is the default here. You can switch the FD to monthly, half-yearly or yearly compounding, or to simple interest paid at maturity.

How to use it

  1. Choose Fixed deposit or Recurring deposit.
  2. For an FD, enter the amount, interest rate, tenure and compounding. For an RD, enter the monthly deposit, interest rate and number of months.
  3. Click Calculate maturity amount.
  4. Read the maturity amount, the total deposited and the interest earned.

Formulas used

Fixed deposit:

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t
P = deposit, r = annual rate as a decimal, n = compounding periods per year, t = years

Recurring deposit (quarterly compounding): each monthly instalment is treated as deposited at the start of the month and compounded for the number of quarters it stays in the account. This matches the standard bank formula M = R × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ [1 − (1 + i)−1/3], where R is the monthly deposit, i is the annual rate ÷ 400 and n is the number of quarters.

Worked examples

FD: ₹1,00,000 at 7% for 5 years with quarterly compounding grows to about ₹1,41,478, so the interest is about ₹41,478. With simple interest at maturity, the same deposit gives ₹1,35,000.

RD: ₹1,000 a month at 6% for 12 months means you deposit ₹12,000 and receive about ₹12,395, an interest of about ₹395.

Frequently asked questions

How is FD maturity amount calculated?

The calculator uses A = P × (1 + r/n)^(n×t), where n is how many times interest is compounded each year. Most banks use quarterly compounding.

How is RD interest calculated?

Each monthly instalment earns interest for the months it remains deposited, with interest compounded quarterly in most banks. The calculator adds up the growth of every instalment.

Which compounding should I choose for an FD?

Choose the option your bank states in its deposit terms. If you are unsure, quarterly is the most common in India.

Why does my bank show a slightly different amount?

Banks can round interest, apply day counts, or use different compounding dates, so small differences are normal.

What happens if I break an FD or RD early?

Most banks charge a penalty and pay a lower rate. Check your deposit terms before withdrawing early.

Is the interest taxable?

Interest from FDs and RDs is generally added to your income and taxed as per your slab. Speak to a tax professional for your situation.
