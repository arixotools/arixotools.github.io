Convert land area units used in Bangladesh and West Bengal

Land in Bengal is measured in units such as katha, bigha, decimal (shotok) and chatak, while plans, maps and buyers from other regions often use square feet, square meters or acres. This converter changes any of these into any other, and also shows the same area in every unit at once so you can compare without repeating the calculation.

How to use it

  1. Type the area you want to convert.
  2. Pick the unit you are converting from and the unit you want to.
  3. Read the answer instantly. Use the swap button to reverse the units.
  4. Check the table below the result to see the area in all other units.

Worked examples

5 katha is 3,600 sq ft, which is about 334.5 sq m or 8.26 decimal. One bigha is 20 katha, about 33 decimal, or roughly 1,337.8 sq m. One acre is 100 decimal and about 60.5 katha.

If a plot is priced per katha and you want the price per square foot, convert 1 katha to square feet (720) and divide the price by that number.

Why the answer can differ on the ground

Bigha and katha are traditional units and their size is not the same everywhere. Different districts, states and countries use different values, and some areas define a bigha as well over 14,400 sq ft. This tool uses the common Bangladesh and West Bengal figures listed above. For a purchase, sale, inheritance or registration, always use the measurement written in your deed or recorded by the local land office, and have the plot measured by a licensed surveyor.

Frequently asked questions

1 katha is how many square feet?

With the common Bangladesh and West Bengal standard used here, 1 katha equals 720 square feet.

1 bigha is how many katha?

1 bigha equals 20 katha in this standard, which is 14,400 square feet.

1 decimal is how many square feet?

1 decimal, also called shotok or cent, equals 435.6 square feet, or one hundredth of an acre.

How many katha are in 1 acre?

An acre is 43,560 square feet, so it is about 60.5 katha at 720 square feet per katha.

Is a bigha the same everywhere?

No. The size of a bigha varies by region, so always confirm the local value before relying on a conversion.

Can I use this for legal land documents?

Use it for quick estimates only. Legal documents should follow the measurements recorded in the deed and by the land authority.
