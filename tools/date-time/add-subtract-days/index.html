Add or Subtract Days From a Date

Add or subtract days, weeks, months or years and see the date it lands on.

About the Add or Subtract Days From a Date

This calculator tells you what date you reach after adding or subtracting a number of days, weeks, months or years. It is handy for deadlines, notice periods, warranty and visa dates, loan terms, delivery estimates and anything else counted from a start date. The start date defaults to today and you can change it freely.

How to use the Add or Subtract Days From a Date

  1. Pick the start date, or keep today's date.
  2. Choose Add or Subtract.
  3. Enter the amount and choose days, weeks, months or years.
  4. Click Calculate to see the exact date and weekday.

Where it is useful

  • Working out a due date, such as 30, 60 or 90 days from today.
  • Calculating notice periods and contract end dates.
  • Finding the date of a milestone, such as 100 days from a start.
  • Checking warranty and subscription expiry dates.

How months and years are handled

Adding months moves the same day number into a later month. If that day does not exist, the result is the last day of that month. For example, 31 January plus 1 month is 28 February in 2026 (29 February in a leap year). Days and weeks are added exactly, so 1 January 2026 plus 100 days is 11 April 2026.

Tips and common mistakes

  • For business days only, use the Working Days Calculator.
  • Dates use the standard calendar, with leap years handled correctly.
  • A start date on the 29th, 30th or 31st may shift when adding months, as explained above.
  • Check legal deadlines with the exact rule that applies, as some count differently.

Frequently asked questions

What is 90 days from today?

Choose today as the start date, enter 90, pick Days and Add. The tool shows the date and weekday.

How do I subtract days from a date?

Pick Subtract, enter the number of days and click Calculate.

What happens when adding months to the 31st?

The result is clamped to the last day of the target month.

Are leap years handled?

Yes.

Does it skip weekends?

No. For working days, use the Working Days Calculator.
