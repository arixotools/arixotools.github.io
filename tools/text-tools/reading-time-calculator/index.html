About the Reading Time Calculator

Readers like to know how long an article will take, and speakers need to know whether a speech fits its slot. This calculator counts the words in your text and divides by a reading speed to estimate the time, then separately estimates how long it would take to read aloud at a normal speaking pace of about 130 words per minute. It also counts characters, sentences and paragraphs.

How to use the Reading Time Calculator

  1. Paste or type your text into the box.
  2. Choose a reading speed: slow, average or fast.
  3. The reading time, speaking time and counts update instantly.
  4. Edit the text and watch the numbers change.

Where it is useful

  • Adding 'X min read' labels to blog posts and articles.
  • Timing speeches, presentations and voice-overs.
  • Checking essay or assignment length.
  • Planning video scripts and podcasts.

How the estimate works

Reading time = words ÷ words per minute

Silent reading speed for adults averages around 200 to 250 words per minute, and public speaking is slower, typically 120 to 150 words per minute. A 1,000-word article takes about 4 minutes 12 seconds at 238 wpm and about 7 minutes 42 seconds to read aloud.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Technical or dense text is read more slowly, so pick a lower speed.
  • Add time for pauses, slides and examples when timing a speech.
  • Reading speed varies a lot between people and languages.
  • Use the Word Counter if you also need exact limits for a form.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to read 1000 words?

About 4 minutes at an average adult reading speed of 238 words per minute.

How many words per minute is average reading speed?

Roughly 200 to 250 words per minute for adults reading silently.

How long is a 5 minute speech in words?

Around 650 words at a speaking pace of about 130 words per minute.

Does it work for Bangla and Hindi text?

Yes, words are counted by spaces. Reading speeds in other languages may differ.

Is my text uploaded?

No. It stays in your browser.
