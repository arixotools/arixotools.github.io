About the Quadratic Equation Solver

A quadratic equation has the form ax² + bx + c = 0 and appears throughout algebra, physics and geometry. This solver applies the quadratic formula to your coefficients and tells you whether the equation has two real roots, one repeated root or two complex roots, together with the discriminant that decides which case you are in.

How to use the Quadratic Equation Solver

  1. Write your equation in the form ax² + bx + c = 0.
  2. Enter the values of a, b and c. Use negative numbers where needed.
  3. Click Solve.
  4. Read the roots and the discriminant.

Where it is useful

  • Algebra homework and exam revision.
  • Projectile motion and area problems.
  • Checking factorisation results.
  • Understanding when an equation has real or complex solutions.

Quadratic formula

x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) ÷ 2a
Discriminant D = b² − 4ac

If D is positive there are two real roots, if D is zero there is one repeated root, and if D is negative the roots are complex. For x² − 5x + 6 = 0, D = 1 and the roots are 3 and 2.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Move every term to one side first so the right-hand side is zero.
  • If a equals zero the equation is linear, not quadratic.
  • Check your answer by substituting a root back into the equation.
  • Complex roots come in a pair, with the same real part and opposite imaginary parts.

Frequently asked questions

What is the discriminant?

It is b² − 4ac. Its sign tells you the type of roots.

What if a is zero?

Then the equation is linear and has just one solution, so the tool shows an error.

What are complex roots?

Roots that contain the imaginary unit i, which occur when the discriminant is negative.

How do I check my roots?

Substitute a root into ax² + bx + c. The result should be 0.

Can I enter decimals?

Yes, decimals and negatives are supported.
