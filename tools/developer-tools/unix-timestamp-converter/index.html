Unix Timestamp Converter

Convert between Unix timestamps and readable dates in UTC and your local time.

Format YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS. Used only if the timestamp box is empty.

About the Unix Timestamp Converter

A Unix timestamp, or epoch time, counts the seconds that have passed since 1 January 1970 at 00:00:00 UTC. Programmers, databases, APIs and log files use it because a single number is easy to store and compare. This converter turns a timestamp into a readable date in UTC and in your own time zone, and turns a date back into a timestamp. It also detects whether your number is in seconds or milliseconds.

How to use the Unix Timestamp Converter

  1. To read a timestamp, type it in the first box and click Convert.
  2. To get a timestamp from a date, leave the first box empty and type the date in UTC.
  3. Read the results in seconds, milliseconds and ISO 8601 format.

Where it is useful

  • Reading dates in logs, databases and API responses.
  • Setting token expiry and scheduling times in code.
  • Comparing events across time zones.
  • Debugging date bugs caused by time zones.

A 10-digit number is normally in seconds, and a 13-digit number is in milliseconds. This tool treats values of 100 billion or more as milliseconds.

Time zones

A Unix timestamp has no time zone. It points to one exact moment in time. The UTC date is the same everywhere, while the local time depends on your device's time zone.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Enter the UTC date and time, not your local time, when converting a date to a timestamp.
  • Negative timestamps represent dates before 1970.
  • Some systems use microseconds, which have 16 digits and are not detected here.
  • Be careful with the year 2038 problem on systems that store seconds in 32 bits.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Unix timestamp?

The number of seconds since 1 January 1970 00:00:00 UTC.

How do I tell seconds from milliseconds?

Seconds are usually 10 digits and milliseconds 13 digits.

What is the current timestamp?

It changes every second. Convert a recent date to see it, or check your system's clock.

Does the timestamp include a time zone?

No. It marks a single moment, and you choose how to display it.

What is the year 2038 problem?

Systems storing seconds in a signed 32-bit number overflow on 19 January 2038.
