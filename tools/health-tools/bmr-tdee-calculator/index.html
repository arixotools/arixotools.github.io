About the BMR and TDEE Calculator

Your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the energy your body uses just to stay alive at rest. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) adds the calories you burn through movement and exercise. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, widely used for estimating resting energy, and multiplies the result by your activity level to estimate how many calories you need to maintain your weight.

How to use the BMR and TDEE Calculator

  1. Choose your sex and enter age, height and weight.
  2. Pick the activity level that best matches your week.
  3. Click Calculate.
  4. Read your BMR and your estimated maintenance calories.

Where it is useful

  • Planning a healthy calorie target with a professional's advice.
  • Understanding why portions and activity matter for weight.
  • Comparing the calories in food with what you burn.
  • Tracking how your needs change as weight and activity change.

Formula and example

Men: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5
Women: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161
TDEE = BMR × activity factor

A 30-year-old man, 175 cm and 70 kg, has a BMR of about 1,649 kcal. With light exercise (×1.375) his TDEE is about 2,267 kcal.

Important

These are population-based estimates. Individual needs vary with muscle mass, medical conditions, medicines, pregnancy and more. This tool gives general information only and is not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before making major changes to your diet, especially if you have a health condition.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Be honest about activity. Most people overestimate it.
  • Recheck your numbers after you gain or lose a significant amount of weight.
  • Do not cut calories sharply without professional guidance.
  • Use the BMI calculator alongside this tool for a fuller picture.

Frequently asked questions

What is BMR?

BMR is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to keep basic functions going.

What is TDEE?

It is your total daily calories burned, including all movement and exercise.

Which equation is used?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

Is this accurate for everyone?

No. It is an estimate and individual needs vary.

Should I eat exactly my TDEE?

TDEE is a rough maintenance level. For weight goals, speak to a doctor or dietitian.
